LLaDA trained a diffusion model from scratch on trillions of tokens. Most dLLMs since then skip that: they start from a pretrained AR model and convert it with a much shorter continued training run. When a paper says a dLLM has **AR initialization**, this is what it means: the weights start as an AR checkpoint, then are trained further as a diffusion model.

## What has to change

An AR checkpoint already knows language. Three things about it do not fit diffusion:

1. **The attention mask.** AR attention is causal; diffusion needs tokens to see context on both sides.
2. **The objective.** Next-token prediction becomes the masked denoising loss (M03).
3. **Where predictions come out.** In an AR model, the output at position $i$ predicts token $i+1$. A masked model normally predicts token $i$ at position $i$.

The mask is the big decision, and it splits the field in two:

## Route 1: full attention

**DiffuLLaMA** (ICLR 2025) converts GPT-2 and LLaMA models up to 7B. Two tricks ease the change: **attention-mask annealing**, which relaxes the causal mask gradually during training instead of switching at once, and **keeping the shift**, so position $i-1$ still predicts token $i$ and the AR output head keeps doing what it was trained for.

**Dream 7B** starts from Qwen2.5-7B, also keeps the shift, and adds a noise schedule that adapts per token to how much context it has.

Both end up as LLaDA-style models: full bidirectional attention, one canvas, no exact KV cache.

## Route 2: block-causal attention

The newer and larger models keep AR structure across blocks and add diffusion only inside a block (M08).

- **SDAR** converts Qwen3 models, dense and MoE, up to 30B-A3B, with a short adaptation stage after AR pretraining.
- **Fast-dLLM v2** converts Qwen2.5 with roughly a billion tokens of fine-tuning (P01).
- **LLaDA 2.0** converts Ant Group's Ling 2.0 MoE models (16B and 100B) with a block-size schedule: start with tiny blocks (1 is plain AR), grow them to the full sequence, then shrink back to a block size that is efficient at inference, such as 32.

<Note>
	Why route 2 wins at scale: the model changes less, so less of the AR knowledge has to be relearned, and inference keeps an exact KV cache across blocks. The cost is that order is left-to-right again between blocks.
</Note>

This is also why the largest dLLMs are MoE models: the strongest AR checkpoints available to convert are MoE. M09 looks at what that does to inference.
